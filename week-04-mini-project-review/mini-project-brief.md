# Mini-Project 1 Brief

**Due:** presented in-session, Week 4. **Team size:** the group you've been working with since Week 2 (3–4 people). **Dataset:** `recent-grads.csv` (Week 2 folder) — you may also pull in one additional public dataset if you want to extend the analysis, but it's optional, not required.

## The ask
Answer a question about college major choice and outcomes that you actually find interesting — not one assigned to you. It can build directly on something your group started in Week 2 or 3, or be a new angle entirely.

## Requirements
- **One clear claim**, stated as a sentence, not just a chart with no conclusion attached.
- **At least one engineered column** — something computed from the raw data, not a raw column read straight off the sheet.
- **A short Excel analysis** showing your work (formulas visible, not just pasted values).
- **A Power BI dashboard** with at least 3 linked visuals and one slicer/filter.
- **A brief acknowledgment of a limitation** — a small sample size, a possible confound, a missing variable. Every real analysis has one; naming yours is part of the deliverable, not an admission of failure.

## What you're being asked to avoid
- A finding that's just a sorted list ("here are the top 5 majors by salary") with no engineered column and no "why does this matter."
- Presenting a correlation as if it settles a causal question.
- Copying the exact demo built in Week 3's session — that was to teach the tool, not to be your answer.

## Presenting
4–5 minutes, dashboard on screen, lead with your claim in the first 30 seconds. See `rubric.md` for what officers (and the room) will be listening for.
