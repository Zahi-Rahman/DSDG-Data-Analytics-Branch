# Week 4: Mini-Project Share-Out & Retrospective

**Format:** Presentations + structured retro · **Length:** ~75 minutes · **Slides:** `slides/week-04-slides.pptx`

This week has no new technical content — it's the payoff for weeks 2–3, and the template every future review week (weeks 8 and 12) will follow.

## What members should have coming in
Each group, working async since Week 3 (or during an optional open lab if your officer team runs one — not scripted here), has:
1. Chosen their own question on `recent-grads.csv` (or a close variant — see `mini-project-brief.md`), building on whatever they explored in Weeks 2–3.
2. At least one engineered column that didn't exist in the raw data.
3. A short Excel analysis **and** a Power BI dashboard.
4. A one-line "so what" — the actual claim, not just a chart.

Full spec and rubric: `mini-project-brief.md` and `rubric.md`. Send both out at the *end* of Week 3, not the start of Week 4 — groups need the intervening time to build.

---

## 1. Share-out (~30 min)
Each group: **4–5 minutes**, dashboard on screen, one clear claim stated out loud in the first 30 seconds — don't let groups bury the finding at the end. Open with: *"Here's what we found, and here's the one number that convinced us."* Q&A is 1–2 questions per group, from the room, not just officers — model asking "what else could explain that" if no one else does.

**This is not a competition.** Say that explicitly before the first group presents. The goal is exposure to 5–6 different approaches to the same dataset — showing members that "creative and correct" isn't one path.

## 2. Retrospective (~20 min)
Whole-branch discussion, not per-group reports. Three prompts, in order:
1. **What felt hard this cycle?** (technical or otherwise)
2. **What's one thing that clicked?**
3. **What do you want more practice on before we move to SQL?**

Officers: actually write these down. This is real input, not a formality — it's your only structured signal before pacing decisions for the SQL block.

## 3. Wrap-up & look ahead (~10 min)
Name the arc explicitly: *"Month one was about being comfortable enough with tools to ask a real question and defend it. Month two is SQL — same instinct, a different way of getting at the data, and it's the single most-requested skill in analyst job postings, so this is where things start compounding toward your resume, not just your GitHub."*
